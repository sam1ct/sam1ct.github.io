My Academic Approach

I start with questions, then follow them into the real world.

I approach social policy through both empirical research and ethical inquiry. Sociology helps me understand how institutions and social structures shape people's experiences, while Humanities gives me space to examine the values, assumptions, and moral choices embedded within those systems.

I am particularly interested in the space between what is possible, what is effective, and what is right. My goal is to bring research into conversation with practice—to understand complex problems carefully, ask better questions, and contribute to institutions that serve people well.

Roots of My Research

  1. Social Policy

    I am interested in how policy decisions distribute opportunity, resources, and responsibility—and in the ethical questions that emerge when policymakers must balance competing needs and values.

  2. Sociology

    I study how social structures, institutions, and inequalities shape people's everyday experiences. Sociology gives me a way to understand the social consequences of policy decisions and the communities those decisions affect.

  3. Government & Communications

    I am interested in how leaders make, justify, and communicate difficult decisions. I study the role of political institutions, public reasoning, and rhetoric in shaping how policies are understood and legitimized.
